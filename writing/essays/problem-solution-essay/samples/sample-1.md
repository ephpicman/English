# Urban traffic congestion: problem-and-solution essay

*Illustrative IELTS-style response; adapt it to the exact prompt.*

## Before writing

The essay identifies why congestion persists and matches each solution to a cause.

## Essay structure

It separates travel demand and weak alternatives, then proposes coordinated transport and planning measures.

## Introduction and problem framing

Traffic congestion is a persistent problem in many growing cities. It wastes time, increases stress, and can worsen local air quality. Although no single policy can remove every delay, cities can reduce congestion by making alternatives to private cars more reliable and by managing road use more effectively.

## Main problem or cause 1

One cause is that public transport may be inconvenient or unreliable. If buses arrive irregularly, routes do not connect residential areas with workplaces, or services stop early in the evening, people who can afford a car may choose to drive. This creates a cycle: more cars slow buses, making public transport even less attractive. The problem is particularly serious for workers whose shifts do not match standard office hours.

## Main problem or cause 2 (if needed)

Urban design can also increase unnecessary car journeys. Housing, shops, schools, and offices may be separated by long distances, while walking and cycling routes are incomplete or unsafe. In such places, even a short errand can require a vehicle. Road widening may provide temporary relief, but it can encourage additional driving and leave the underlying travel pattern unchanged.

## Solutions matched to causes

Cities should first improve the reliability, frequency, and coverage of public transport, including connections between outer neighbourhoods and major routes. Dedicated bus lanes can help services avoid some congestion, while clear real-time information makes journeys easier to plan. For shorter trips, continuous pavements, safe crossings, and protected cycle routes can provide realistic alternatives. Planning authorities can also encourage mixed-use neighbourhoods where everyday services are closer to homes. Measures such as parking charges may be appropriate in busy centres, but should be introduced alongside affordable alternatives so that people with limited choices are not unfairly burdened.

## Conclusion

Congestion results from both transport choices and the way cities are designed. Improving public transport, safer active-travel routes, and better local planning addresses these causes more directly than relying on road expansion alone.
