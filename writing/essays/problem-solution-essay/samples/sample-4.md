# Stress among university students: problem-and-solution essay

*Illustrative IELTS-style response; adapt recommendations to the institution and student group.*

## Before writing

The response distinguishes workload pressures from uncertainty about support and matches each cause with practical institutional changes.

## Essay structure

It explains two sources of stress and proposes actions that students and institutions can take.

## Introduction and problem framing

Many university students experience stress as they balance coursework, deadlines, finances, and personal responsibilities. Some pressure can motivate preparation, but prolonged stress can interfere with sleep, concentration, and participation. Reducing the problem requires attention to both academic demands and the support available to students.

## Main problem or cause 1

One cause is the uneven distribution of deadlines. If several large assignments are due in the same week, students may feel forced to prioritise submission over understanding. Students who also work part-time or care for family members have less flexibility to absorb unexpected changes. Unclear assessment criteria can add to this pressure because learners may spend time guessing what a task requires.

## Main problem or cause 2 (if needed)

A second cause is that students may not know where to seek help until difficulties become severe. Support information can be spread across different websites, and some students may worry that asking for help will be viewed negatively. Financial concerns, isolation, and unfamiliar academic expectations can compound these difficulties, especially for students who are new to the institution or country.

## Solutions matched to causes

Departments can coordinate major deadlines across modules, publish assessment calendars early, and provide clear rubrics with examples of successful work. Tutors can teach practical planning methods and encourage students to raise workload conflicts before a deadline is missed. Institutions should also make support routes easy to find, explain confidentiality and its limits, and offer several ways to make contact. These measures do not replace professional care when it is needed, but they can reduce avoidable uncertainty and make early help-seeking easier. Students can use peer study groups and realistic weekly plans, while recognising that structural problems cannot be solved by individual time management alone.

## Conclusion

Student stress is influenced by both workload design and access to support. Coordinated deadlines, clearer expectations, and visible support services can reduce avoidable pressure while helping students respond earlier when they need assistance.
