# Printed books and e-books: compare-and-contrast essay

*Illustrative IELTS-style response; adapt it to the exact question and audience.*

## Before writing

Compare the formats by reading experience and practical access rather than treating one as universally superior.

## Essay structure

The essay applies the same criteria to both formats and weighs their implications.

## Introduction and comparison basis

Printed books and e-books provide access to the same kinds of writing, but the reading experience can differ. Comparing portability and concentration shows why readers may prefer different formats for different purposes.

## Criterion 1: both subjects

E-books allow readers to carry a large library on one device and adjust font size, which can be helpful for travel or for people who need larger text. Digital search functions also make it easier to locate a term or passage. Printed books require physical space and can be heavier, but they do not depend on battery life or a compatible device. They can also be shared without transferring a digital file, although access still depends on borrowing or buying a copy.

## Criterion 2: both subjects

The formats can influence concentration and study habits. Some readers find printed pages easier to navigate and prefer making handwritten notes in the margins. Others value digital highlighting and the ability to search notes across several texts. Reading on a device can introduce distractions if notifications or other applications are nearby, while a printed book can encourage a single-task environment. Nevertheless, a dedicated e-reader and sensible settings may reduce these distractions.

## Synthesis and significance

The more useful format depends on the purpose. A student comparing many sources may benefit from digital search, while a reader who wants a screen-free evening may prefer print. Accessibility, cost, and library availability can matter as much as personal preference.

## Conclusion

Neither printed books nor e-books are inherently better for every reader. Their relative strengths depend on context, accessibility needs, and whether portability, annotation, or a distraction-free experience is the main priority.
