# University tuition fees: argumentative essay

*Illustrative IELTS-style response; adapt the position to the precise wording of the question.*

## Before writing

Position: higher education should be affordable, with financial support designed to prevent cost from excluding capable students.

## Essay structure

The essay explains the public value of education, considers funding pressures, and responds to the argument that students should pay the full cost.

## Introduction and thesis

Whether university students should pay high tuition fees is a difficult question because higher education benefits both individuals and society. In my view, governments and institutions should keep education financially accessible, using a mix of public funding and carefully designed contributions rather than allowing fees to become a major barrier.

## Main argument 1

A university education can develop specialist knowledge, analytical skills, and the ability to contribute to complex workplaces. Society also benefits when trained professionals are available in areas such as teaching, research, engineering, and healthcare. If fees are too high, students from lower-income households may avoid suitable courses even when they have the ability to succeed. Scholarships and needs-based grants can reduce this barrier, while transparent information helps applicants understand the likely cost before committing.

## Main argument 2

Nevertheless, universities need reliable funding for teaching, facilities, student support, and research. Asking every student to contribute nothing may place unrealistic pressure on public budgets, especially when demand for places grows. A balanced funding model can include public investment, targeted assistance, and income-based repayment arrangements where these exist. The important principle is that payment terms should not force students to take on risks they cannot reasonably understand or manage.

## Counterargument and response

Some argue that graduates receive higher earning potential and should therefore cover the full cost of their degrees. However, financial returns vary by subject, region, and personal circumstances, and the wider social value of education is not captured by an individual salary. A system based only on private benefit overlooks these broader gains.

## Conclusion

Tuition policy should protect access while ensuring universities can deliver quality education. Combining public support with fair, transparent contributions is more defensible than either unlimited fees or a funding model that ignores the real cost of teaching.
