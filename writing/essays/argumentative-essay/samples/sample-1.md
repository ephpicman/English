# Remote work: argumentative essay

*Illustrative IELTS-style response; adapt it to the exact prompt supplied in an exam.*

## Before writing

Position: remote work is beneficial for suitable roles, but it needs deliberate management to protect collaboration and boundaries.

## Essay structure

The argument gives two benefits, acknowledges a limitation, and explains why a balanced policy is preferable.

## Introduction and thesis

Remote work has changed how many organisations think about productivity and staff wellbeing. Although working from home is not practical for every job, I believe it offers important advantages for roles that can be performed remotely, provided employers set clear expectations and support communication.

## Main argument 1

The first advantage is the time and flexibility remote work can give employees. Commuting may consume a substantial part of the day, leaving less time for family responsibilities, rest, or personal development. Removing that journey can also make employment more accessible to people who live far from a city centre or have mobility needs that make daily travel difficult. For tasks requiring sustained concentration, a quiet home environment may reduce interruptions. These benefits are not automatic, since some employees lack suitable workspace, but employers can address this by offering equipment support and allowing staff to choose an appropriate working arrangement.

## Main argument 2

Remote work can also help organisations recruit beyond a narrow geographical area. A company may find a specialist whose skills are unavailable locally, while employees can remain in their communities rather than relocating. In addition, written updates and shared project records can make decisions easier to trace. This works best when managers judge outcomes rather than simply measuring online presence, and when teams agree how quickly colleagues should respond.

## Counterargument and response

Critics reasonably point out that remote workers can miss informal learning and feel isolated. New employees may find it harder to ask quick questions, and video meetings cannot replace every kind of face-to-face interaction. However, these problems support a planned hybrid approach or scheduled mentoring rather than a blanket rejection of remote work.

## Conclusion

Remote work is most effective when it matches the job and the employee’s circumstances. With clear communication, fair access to equipment, and intentional opportunities for collaboration, its flexibility can benefit both staff and employers.
