# Online discussion: Should every meeting have an agenda?

*Illustrative response; adapt it to the actual discussion and other participants’ contributions.*

## Direct response

Most meetings benefit from an agenda, although it can be brief and informal rather than a detailed schedule.

## Reasoning and support

An agenda gives participants a shared purpose and helps them prepare the information needed for a decision. It also makes it easier to notice when the discussion has moved away from the issue the group needs to resolve. For recurring meetings, a short list of decisions and open actions may be enough.

## Example or qualification

For example, a 15-minute project check-in may need only three items: blockers, decisions required, and next steps. A brainstorming session should leave more room for ideas that emerge during the conversation.

## Engage another view

I understand the concern that agendas can make meetings feel rigid. That is a risk when the agenda is treated as a script rather than a guide, but a facilitator can adapt it when a new issue genuinely matters.

## Synthesis or question

The useful question is not whether an agenda exists, but whether it helps people use the time well.
