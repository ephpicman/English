# Proposal to introduce a shared service-request queue

*Illustrative business proposal. Verify operational facts, costs, and approvals before using it as a decision document.*

## Before you draft

The decision requested is approval for a limited pilot, not an immediate organisation-wide rollout.

## Draft structure

The proposal describes the current problem, the proposed queue, delivery controls, and the evidence needed to decide whether to expand it.

## Title and decision metadata

**Owner:** Operations lead  
**Date:** 24 October 2026  
**Decision requested:** Approve a four-week pilot

## Executive summary

Approve a four-week pilot of one shared service-request queue for facilities and office-support requests. The pilot would make ownership, status, due date, and next action visible in one place. Existing systems would remain in use during the trial, and the team would review the results before deciding whether broader implementation is justified.

## Business need and current state

Requests currently arrive through several channels. This makes it difficult to identify duplicates and determine who is responsible for follow-up. The problem should be confirmed through a review of recent requests before the pilot begins.

## Proposed solution and scope

Use an existing approved platform to record new requests with a reference number, category, owner, status, next action, and due date. The pilot covers two request types and excludes urgent safety incidents, which must continue through the established escalation route.

## Market, customer, or operational rationale

A shared queue may reduce avoidable handovers and improve the requester’s ability to see whether an item is being handled. It should not be presented as a guaranteed time saving before the pilot is evaluated.

## Delivery plan and governance

The operations lead will configure the fields, explain the process, and review usage weekly. The service manager will approve any change to scope. The pilot ends with a review meeting and a documented recommendation.

## Budget, benefits, and commercial assumptions

The pilot uses existing software, so no new licence purchase is proposed. Staff time for configuration and review is the main cost and should be recorded. Any later procurement would require a separate business case.

## Risks and mitigations

Risks include duplicate entry, inconsistent status use, and sensitive information being placed in the queue. Provide clear field definitions and restrict access according to policy.

## Success measures and decision requested

Review completeness of ownership and next-action fields, user feedback, and examples of avoidable follow-up. Approve the pilot and name a sponsor to review the findings after four weeks.

## Language and quality review

Check the scope, cost assumptions, and approval route before circulation.
