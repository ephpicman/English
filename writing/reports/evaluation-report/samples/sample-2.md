# Evaluation of a staff onboarding guide

*Illustrative evaluation report. Confirm feedback and usability evidence before drawing conclusions.*

## Before drafting

The evaluation assesses whether new staff can use the guide to complete common first-week tasks. It does not assess the entire onboarding programme.

## Report structure

The report sets criteria, describes the evidence needed, interprets results cautiously, and recommends improvements.

## Title and document control

- **Evaluator:** People Operations
- **Period:** October–November 2026
- **Status:** Illustrative evaluation
- **Audience:** Onboarding programme owner

## Executive summary

The guide should be considered effective only if new starters can locate key information and complete the intended tasks, not merely because the document has been published. A short usability test and follow-up feedback can reveal whether the structure and links are clear.

## Purpose, questions, and criteria

The evaluation asks whether the guide is easy to navigate, accurate, accessible, and sufficient for routine first-week tasks. Criteria include task completion, clarity of instructions, working links, and the number and type of questions that remain after reading.

## Method and evidence base

Invite a small group of recent starters to locate specific information using the guide without verbal assistance. Record where they hesitate, then ask which instructions were unclear. Check links and policy references with their owners. Report the number of participants and limitations from the actual test rather than generalising beyond the sample.

## Results against criteria

- **Navigation:** Record whether participants can locate the correct section.
- **Accuracy:** Confirm policy owners have approved the linked guidance.
- **Accessibility:** Review document structure and test the format used by staff.
- **Task support:** Note questions that remain unresolved after using the guide.

## Interpretation and limitations

A small usability test can reveal obvious barriers but cannot represent every role or access need. A guide may also become outdated after a policy change. Results should be treated as evidence for revision, not a guarantee that onboarding is complete.

## Overall judgment

The guide is ready for broader use only after policy checks and usability findings have been addressed. It should remain a support resource rather than a replacement for direct guidance from managers.

## Recommendations and follow-up

Revise unclear sections, assign an owner for each linked policy, and schedule a review after the next intake. Reuse the same task prompts to compare whether navigation improves.
