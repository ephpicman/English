# Analysis of alternatives for staff onboarding delivery

*Illustrative analytical report. Confirm organisational requirements, staff feedback, and delivery costs before deciding.*

## Before drafting

The analysis compares in-person, online, and blended onboarding against accessibility, consistency, staff time, and opportunity for questions.

## Report structure

It defines the decision, summarises the relevant factors, compares options, and recommends a trial rather than assuming one format suits everyone.

## Title and document control

- **Owner:** People Operations
- **Scope:** First-week onboarding
- **Status:** Illustrative analysis
- **Audience:** People Operations manager

## Executive summary

A blended onboarding model may offer a useful balance between consistent information and direct support. Routine policies can be shared in a structured online guide, while discussion and role-specific questions can be handled in live sessions. The recommendation should be tested with new starters and managers before it becomes the default.

## Purpose, scope, and method

The review considers delivery format for general first-week information, not specialist safety or technical training. Relevant evidence includes new-starter feedback, manager workload, completion records, and accessibility needs. The organisation should verify these sources and check any mandatory in-person requirements before changing the programme.

## Findings

- Repeating the same introductory information in multiple live sessions takes staff time.
- New starters still need opportunities to ask questions about their role and team.
- Written material can be revisited, but outdated links or unclear instructions reduce its value.
- Staff may have different access needs and learning preferences.

## Analysis and implications

A fully in-person model supports immediate discussion but can be difficult to schedule consistently. A fully online model offers flexibility and repeat access but may leave questions unresolved. A blended approach can separate standard information from interactive support, provided the online materials are accessible and maintained. The model should not shift the burden onto new employees to interpret unclear policies without help.

## Options and criteria

**Option A: Keep all sessions in person.** Strong interaction but high scheduling demand.

**Option B: Move all content online.** Flexible and consistent, but weaker for questions and social connection.

**Option C: Put stable information online and retain live discussion for questions and team-specific context.** Balanced, but requires clear ownership of both components.

Option C best matches the mixed purpose of onboarding.

## Conclusion

The best delivery model distinguishes information that can be revisited from support that benefits from conversation.

## Recommendations and next steps

Pilot the blended format with the next intake, collect feedback from participants and managers, and review completion and question patterns. Keep an accessible alternative for anyone who cannot use the standard digital format.
