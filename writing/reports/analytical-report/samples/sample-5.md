# Analysis of maintenance scheduling options

*Illustrative analytical report. Use actual asset records, safety requirements, and maintenance history before implementation.*

## Before drafting

Define the assets in scope, the current maintenance policy, and what counts as an urgent failure. The example illustrates decision logic, not a technical recommendation for a specific site.

## Report structure

The report compares reactive, calendar-based, and risk-based scheduling and identifies the evidence needed for a decision.

## Title and document control

- **Owner:** Facilities operations
- **Scope:** Routine equipment maintenance
- **Status:** Illustrative analysis
- **Audience:** Facilities manager

## Executive summary

A risk-based schedule may be more effective than treating every asset identically, but it depends on reliable maintenance records and a clear assessment of safety and operational impact. The recommended approach is to retain mandatory service intervals and pilot risk-based prioritisation for eligible assets.

## Purpose, scope, and method

The analysis considers how to plan routine checks for equipment with different levels of criticality. It does not override manufacturer guidance, legal obligations, or specialist engineering advice. The actual decision should use verified failure history, asset condition, operating context, and the consequences of downtime.

## Findings

- Some equipment has mandatory inspection intervals.
- The consequences of failure vary between assets.
- Maintenance records differ in completeness.
- Urgent repairs can displace planned work when no priority rules are agreed.

## Analysis and implications

Reactive maintenance can reduce planned work on low-risk assets but may cause avoidable disruption when a failure has significant consequences. A uniform calendar can be easier to administer but may allocate the same attention to assets with very different risks. Risk-based scheduling can focus effort where failure matters most, but poor data may create false confidence. Missing records should be treated as uncertainty requiring review, not as evidence that an asset is low risk.

## Options and criteria

**Option A: Continue reactive maintenance where permitted.** Low planning effort but potentially unpredictable disruption.

**Option B: Use fixed intervals for all assets.** Simple to manage but may not reflect differing consequences.

**Option C: Retain required intervals and prioritise additional checks by risk.** More targeted but needs asset data and clear governance.

Option C is preferred for further assessment, subject to technical and compliance review.

## Conclusion

Maintenance planning should balance reliability, cost, and the consequence of failure rather than optimise for one factor alone.

## Recommendations and next steps

Validate the asset register, confirm mandatory intervals with qualified personnel, and test a risk-ranking method on a small group of assets. Review the result before changing the wider maintenance schedule.
