## Motivation and direction

I am interested in business analytics because organisations need to make decisions from data without losing sight of the people and processes behind the numbers. I want to understand how data is collected, checked, interpreted, and communicated so that analysis can support a useful decision rather than simply produce a chart. My goal is to develop strong technical foundations while learning to explain uncertainty and limitations to audiences who may not have a technical background.

## Relevant preparation

My interest grew through coursework involving spreadsheets, statistics, and research methods. I enjoyed the process of turning a broad question into a set of variables that could be examined, but I also learned how easily a result can be misleading when the definitions are unclear. In a project using a sample retail dataset, I compared monthly transactions and noticed that the final period was incomplete. The initial chart suggested a decline, but the partial period made the comparison unreliable. I revised the chart to identify the limitation and explained what additional data would be needed before drawing a conclusion.

## Skills and learning evidence

That project strengthened my attention to data quality and reproducibility. I documented the cleaning steps, checked for duplicate identifiers, and kept a record of assumptions so that another student could follow the analysis. I have also practised using spreadsheet functions and introductory SQL to filter records, group results, and compare categories. When I encounter an unfamiliar method, I try to test it on a small example before applying it to a larger dataset. I know that my current technical skills are still developing, but I have learned to ask precise questions, check results, and avoid presenting a guess as a finding.

Working in a group has also taught me to communicate the meaning of an analysis, not only the method. A chart is useful when its labels, time period, and units make sense to the reader. I want to continue improving this skill alongside more advanced analytical methods.

## Fit with opportunity

I am seeking a programme that combines quantitative reasoning, database skills, and practical case work. I would like to learn how to select appropriate analytical methods, validate data, and evaluate whether a conclusion is supported by the available evidence. The actual programme’s modules, projects, and assessment approach should be checked before this statement is submitted so that the fit can be explained accurately rather than through generic praise.

## Goals and contribution

In the short term, I want to strengthen my skills in SQL, statistical reasoning, and data visualisation. Later, I hope to work in an analyst role where I can help teams understand operational patterns and evaluate possible changes. I would contribute a careful approach to group projects, clear documentation, and a willingness to ask whether the data answers the question being asked. I am particularly interested in learning from classmates who approach business problems from different professional or academic backgrounds.

## Conclusion

Business analytics appeals to me because it connects technical work with practical decisions. I am ready to develop my skills through structured study and to approach analysis with curiosity, accuracy, and respect for the limits of evidence.
