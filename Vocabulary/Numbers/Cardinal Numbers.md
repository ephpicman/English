# Cardinal Numbers in British English

## 1. Introduction

**Pronunciation model:** Standard Southern British English (SSBE)  
**Transcription system:** International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)

This guide explains how to read, write, and pronounce cardinal numbers in British English, from basic numbers to very large numbers. It includes IPA transcriptions, word stress, sentence stress, spelling rules, and common pronunciation mistakes.

**Important:** IPA transcriptions represent a standard pronunciation, but actual speech varies slightly by speaker, accent, and speaking speed.

## 4. The Teen-versus-Ty Contrast

One of the most important pronunciation distinctions is the difference between numbers ending in **-teen** and numbers ending in **-ty**.

| Teen number | IPA | Ty number | IPA |
|---|---|---|---|
| thirteen | /ˌθɜːˈtiːn/ | thirty | /ˈθɜːti/ |
| fourteen | /ˌfɔːˈtiːn/ | forty | /ˈfɔːti/ |
| fifteen | /ˌfɪfˈtiːn/ | fifty | /ˈfɪfti/ |
| sixteen | /ˌsɪkˈstiːn/ | sixty | /ˈsɪksti/ |
| seventeen | /ˌsevənˈtiːn/ | seventy | /ˈsevənti/ |
| eighteen | /ˌeɪˈtiːn/ | eighty | /ˈeɪti/ |
| nineteen | /ˌnaɪnˈtiːn/ | ninety | /ˈnaɪnti/ |

### How to make the distinction clear

1. **Teen numbers:** Give the final syllable the main stress when saying the number in isolation.
2. **Ty numbers:** Give the first syllable the main stress.
3. **Vowel length:** The /iː/ in *-teen* is long. The final /i/ in *-ty* is generally shorter.
4. **Sentence context:** Contrastive emphasis can change the stress pattern, so use both stress and context to make the intended number clear.

Practise the pairs slowly first, then at a natural speed:

- thirteen — thirty
- fourteen — forty
- fifteen — fifty
- eighteen — eighty

## 8. Reading Long Numbers: The Three-Digit Grouping Method

The easiest way to read a long cardinal number is to divide it into groups of three digits, starting from the right.

For example:

**7,284,619,053**

| Group | Value | Read as |
|---|---:|---|
| Billions | 7 | seven billion |
| Millions | 284 | two hundred and eighty-four million |
| Thousands | 619 | six hundred and nineteen thousand |
| Units | 053 | fifty-three |

The complete number is:

**Seven billion two hundred and eighty-four million six hundred and nineteen thousand and fifty-three.**

Follow these steps:

1. Separate the digits into groups of three.
2. Name each group: billion, million, thousand, and the final units.
3. Read each non-zero group as a number from one to 999.
4. Add the appropriate scale word.
5. Skip groups containing only zeros.
6. Use *and* naturally within the hundreds groups, following British usage.

Another example:

**406,020,008**

Read it as:

**Four hundred and six million twenty thousand and eight.**

The zero groups do not need to be spoken as *zero thousand* or *zero hundred*.

## 12. Sentence Stress with Cardinal Numbers

Word stress tells you which syllable is prominent inside a word. Sentence stress tells you which word receives the strongest emphasis in a sentence.

Numbers often receive sentence stress when the quantity is important.

- The total is **THIRteen**, not thirty.
- The village has **FIFTY** houses.
- The population is **TWO million**.
- It costs **FOUR hundred** pounds.

The capital letters above show emphasis, not a different spelling or a different IPA transcription.

### Contrastive emphasis

When correcting someone, the stress may shift to highlight the difference.

- “Is it thirty?” — “No, **THIRteen**.”
- “Did you say forty?” — “No, **FOURteen**.”

In normal conversation, the exact intonation depends on what the speaker wants to emphasise. The isolated-word stress patterns are a useful starting point, not a rule that overrides every sentence context.

## 13. Common Pronunciation Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Confusing -teen and -ty**

Give the final syllable of *thirteen* its main stress when saying it on its own. Give the first syllable of *thirty* the main stress.

**Mistake 2: Replacing /θ/ with /t/ or /s/**

In *three*, *thirteen*, *thirty*, and *thousand*, the initial sound is /θ/. Place the tongue lightly between the teeth and let air pass.

**Mistake 3: Pronouncing every written vowel fully**

Unstressed syllables often contain /ə/. For example, the final syllable of *thousand* is weak: /ˈθaʊzənd/.

**Mistake 4: Adding an unnecessary plural ending**

Say *five million*, not *five millions*, when a precise number comes before *million*.

**Mistake 5: Forgetting hyphens in written numbers**

Use *twenty-one*, *thirty-six*, and *ninety-nine*.

## 14. Final Checklist

Before you consider your pronunciation accurate, check that you can:

- Distinguish *thirteen* from *thirty*.
- Pronounce /θ/ clearly in *three*, *thirty*, and *thousand*.
- Put the main stress on the correct syllable.
- Read numbers in groups of three digits.
- Use *and* naturally in British number names.
- Pronounce *hundred*, *thousand*, *million*, and *billion* with appropriate stress.
- Read large numbers without saying every zero.
- Use *million*, *billion*, and *thousand* correctly after specific numbers.

**The most effective strategy:** Learn the basic numbers and tens first, practise the *-teen/-ty* contrast, and then apply the same pronunciation patterns to hundreds, thousands, millions, and billions.
