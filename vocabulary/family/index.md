# Family

**family**: `/ˈfæməli/` - `n. [C]`  
A group of people who are related to each other, especially parents and their children.
  - The whole family gathered for his birthday.

**[immediate family](./immediate-family.md)**: `/ɪˌmiːdiət ˈfæməli/` - `n. [C]`  
Your closest family members, usually your parents, siblings, spouse and children.
  - Only immediate family members attended the small ceremony.

**[nuclear family](./immediate-family.md)**: `/ˌnjuːkliə ˈfæməli/` - `n. [C]`  
A family consisting of parents and their children.
  - The report focused on households made up of a nuclear family.

**blended family**: `/ˌblendɪd ˈfæməli/` - `n. [C]`  
A family formed when adults with children from previous relationships form a new family.
  - Their blended family includes children from both of their previous marriages.

**stepfamily**: `/ˈstepfæməli/` - `n. [C]`  
A family in which one or both adults have children from an earlier relationship.
  - The counsellor helped the stepfamily adjust to living together.

**[extended family](./extended-family.md)**: `/ɪkˌstendɪd ˈfæməli/` - `n. [C]`  
A family that includes relatives beyond parents and children.
  - Our extended family meets every summer.

**[in-laws](./in-laws.md)**: `/ˈɪn lɔːz/` - `n. [pl.]`  
Your relatives by marriage, especially the family of your husband or wife.
  - We are spending Christmas with my in-laws this year.

**in-law**: `/ˈɪn lɔː/` - `n. [C]`  
A relative by marriage.
  - My brother-in-law lives in Leeds.

**[adoptive family](./adoptive-family.md)**: `/əˈdɒptɪv ˈfæməli/` - `n. [C]`  
A family that has legally adopted a child who is not the biological child of the adoptive parent or parents.
  - Her adoptive family encouraged her to learn about her origins.

**[foster family](./foster-family.md)**: `/ˈfɒstə fæməli/` - `n. [C]`  
A family that provides a home for a child who cannot live with their own parents.
  - The child felt safe and welcome with his foster family.

**[parenthood](./parenthood.md)**: `/ˈpeərənthʊd/` - `n. [U]`  
The state of being a parent.
  - They found that parenthood changed their priorities.

**[parentless](./parentless.md)**: `/ˈpeərəntləs/` - `adj.`  
Having no parents.
  - The charity provides support for parentless children.

**[guardianship](./guardianship.md)**: `/ˈɡɑːdiənʃɪp/` - `n. [U]`  
The legal responsibility for caring for a person or managing their affairs, or for their property.
  - Her aunt took on legal guardianship of the child.

**[household](./household.md)**: `/ˈhaʊshəʊld/` - `n. [C]`  
All the people who live together in one home.
  - Everyone in the household shares the cooking.

**[family authority](./family-authority.md)**: `/ˈfæməli ɔːˈθɒrəti/` - `n. [U]`  
The power or right to make decisions and exercise control within a family.
  - Family authority was traditionally held by the oldest man in their community.

**relative**: `/ˈrelətɪv/` - `n. [C]`  
A person who is related to you by blood or marriage.
  - A distant relative invited us to the wedding.

**relatives**: `/ˈrelətɪvz/` - `n. [pl.]`  
People who are related to you.
  - We stayed with relatives during our trip.

**relation**: `/rɪˈleɪʃn/` - `n. [C]`  
A person who belongs to the same family as you.
  - A relation from Australia came to visit.

**kin**: `/kɪn/` - `n. [pl.]`  
People who are related to you.
  - He invited all his kin to the celebration.

**kinship**: `/ˈkɪnʃɪp/` - `n. [U]`  
The relationship between members of the same family or group.
  - The story explores kinship and loyalty within the family.

**next of kin**: `/ˌnekst əv ˈkɪn/` - `n. [sing. or pl.]`  
The person or people most closely related to someone, especially for official purposes or in an emergency.
  - Please provide the name of your next of kin in case of an emergency.

**family-oriented**: `/ˌfæməli ˈɔːrientɪd/` - `adj.`  
Giving importance to family life and family activities.
  - They chose a family-oriented neighbourhood with parks and good schools.

**family-friendly**: `/ˌfæməli ˈfrendli/` - `adj.`  
Suitable for families, including children.
  - The hotel is family-friendly and has activities for children.

**[family history](./family-history.md)**: `/ˌfæməli ˈhɪstri/` - `n. [C, U]`  
Information about a family's members and their lives in the past.
  - She recorded her family's history in a book.

**family background**: `/ˌfæməli ˈbækɡraʊnd/` - `n. [C, U]`  
The circumstances and experiences of the family in which someone grew up.
  - His family background influenced his choice of career.

**family values**: `/ˌfæməli ˈvæljuːz/` - `n. [pl.]`  
The beliefs and principles that a family considers important.
  - They wanted to raise their children according to their family values.

**[family identity](./family-identity.md)**: `/ˈfæməli aɪˈdentəti/` - `n. [C, U]`  
The sense of identity, values and characteristics shared by a family.
  - Shared traditions helped strengthen their family identity.

**family ties**: `/ˌfæməli ˈtaɪz/` - `n. [pl.]`  
The relationships and connections between family members.
  - Even after moving abroad, they maintained strong family ties.
