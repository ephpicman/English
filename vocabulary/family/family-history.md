# Generation

**generation**: `/ˌdʒenəˈreɪʃn/` - `n. [C]`  
All the people of a similar age within a family or society.

**lineage**: `/ˈlɪniɪdʒ/` - `n. [C, U]`  
The series of family members from whom a person is descended.

**lineal**: `/ˈlɪniəl/` - `adj.`  
Related directly to someone's ancestors or descendants.

**inheritance**: `/ɪnˈherɪtəns/` - `n. [C, U]`  
Money, property or characteristics received from a family member.

**inherited**: `/ɪnˈherɪtɪd/` - `adj.`  
Received from a parent or ancestor.

**heir**: `/eə(r)/` - `n. [C]`  
A person who has the legal right to receive another person's money or property after their death.

**heiress**: `/ˈeərəs/` - `n. [C]`  
A woman who has the legal right to inherit money or property.

**heirloom**: `/ˈeəluːm/` - `n. [C]`  
A valuable object that has belonged to a family for many years and is passed from one generation to another.

**legacy**: `/ˈleɡəsi/` - `n. [C]`  
Money or property left to someone in a will; something passed on from the past.

**will**: `/wɪl/` - `n. [C]`  
A legal document stating what should happen to someone's money and property after their death.

**estate**: `/ɪˈsteɪt/` - `n. [C]`  
All the money and property that someone owns, especially at the time of their death.

**biological**: `/ˌbaɪəˈlɒdʒɪkl/` - `adj.`  
Connected with living organisms or with a person's birth parentage.

**genetic**: `/dʒəˈnetɪk/` - `adj.`  
Related to genes or inherited characteristics.

**hereditary**: `/həˈredɪtri/` - `adj.`  
Passed from parents to children through genes.

**heredity**: `/həˈredəti/` - `n. [U]`  
The process by which characteristics are passed from parents to children through genes.

**inherit**: `/ɪnˈherɪt/` - `v. [T]`  
To receive money, property or characteristics from a family member, usually after their death or through genes.

**family resemblance**: `/ˌfæməli rɪˈzembləns/` - `n. [C, U]`  
Similarity in appearance between people who belong to the same family.

**genealogy**: `/ˌdʒiːniˈælədʒi/` - `n. [U]`  
The study or tracing of family history.

**genealogist**: `/ˌdʒiːniˈælədʒɪst/` - `n. [C]`  
A person who studies or traces family histories.

**generational**: `/ˌdʒenəˈreɪʃənl/` - `adj.`  
Related to different generations of a family or society.

**intergenerational**: `/ˌɪntədʒenəˈreɪʃənl/` - `adj.`  
Existing or occurring between different generations.

**familial**: `/fəˈmɪliəl/` - `adj.`  
Related to a family or shared by members of a family.

**familial**: `/fəˈmɪliəl/` - `adj.`  
Occurring among or characteristic of members of the same family.

**family tree**: `/ˌfæməli ˈtriː/` - `n. [C]`  
A diagram showing the relationships between members of a family over several generations.

**lineal descendant**: `/ˌlɪniəl dɪˈsendənt/` - `n. [C]`  
A person who descends directly from someone, such as a child, grandchild or great-grandchild.

**ancestor**: `/ˈænsestə(r)/` - `n. [C]`  
A person in your family who lived a long time ago.

**ancestry**: `/ˈænsestri/` - `n. [U]`  
The members of your family who lived in the past.

**descendant**: `/dɪˈsendənt/` - `n. [C]`  
A person who is related to someone who lived in the past and is descended from them.

**consanguinity**: `/ˌkɒnsæŋˈɡwɪnəti/` - `n. [U]`  
The relationship between people who are related by blood.

**consanguineous**: `/ˌkɒnsæŋˈɡwɪniəs/` - `adj.`  
Related by blood.
