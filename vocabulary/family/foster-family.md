# Foster Family

**foster**: `/ˈfɒstə(r)/` - `v. [T]`  
To care for a child in your home, usually temporarily, without becoming the child's adoptive parent.
  - They agreed to foster a child until a permanent placement could be arranged.
  - They agreed to **foster a child** for six months.
  - The couple decided to **foster kittens** while the shelter was full.

**foster parent**: `/ˈfɒstə peərənt/` - `n. [C]`  
A person who provides a home and care for a child who is not their own, usually temporarily.
  - A foster parent provided a safe home while the child's case was reviewed.
  - The child was placed with **approved foster parents**.

**foster child**: `/ˈfɒstə tʃaɪld/` - `n. [C]`  
A child who is being cared for by a foster parent or foster family.
  - The foster child stayed with the same family for two years.

## Practice: Dialogue, Monologue and Reading

### 1. Dialogue: Becoming a Foster Family

**Priya:** I heard you've decided to **foster** a child.

**James:** Yes. We're going to become **foster parents**. A social worker will help us prepare.

**Priya:** Does that mean the child will be adopted?

**James:** Not necessarily. A **foster child** usually lives with a family temporarily while plans are made for their care. Fostering isn't the same as adoption.

**Priya:** That sounds like a big responsibility.

**James:** It is, so we're learning how to provide a stable, caring home and work with the professionals involved.

### 2. Monologue: Our Experience of Fostering

My partner and I decided to **foster** because we wanted to offer a safe and supportive home to a child who needed one. As **foster parents**, we understand that our role may be temporary and that the child's needs must come first. A **foster child** may have experienced uncertainty, so patience, consistency, and respect are essential. We don't see fostering as a way to replace a child's birth family; we see it as providing care while professionals and family members work toward the most suitable long-term plan.

### 3. Reading: What Fostering Means

To **foster** a child is to care for them in your home, usually on a temporary basis, without automatically becoming their adoptive parent. A **foster parent** provides day-to-day care and support, while a **foster child** is the child receiving that care. The length and purpose of a foster placement vary according to the child's circumstances and the relevant legal system. Fostering may involve working with social workers, schools, health professionals, and the child's family. The central aim is to provide safety, stability, and appropriate support—not to assume that every foster placement will lead to adoption.
