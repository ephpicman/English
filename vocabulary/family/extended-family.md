# Extended Family

## Grandparents

**grandparent**: `/ˈɡrænpeərənt/` - `n. [C]`  
  - She spends the school holidays with her **grandparents**.
  - Both **maternal grandparents** attended the ceremony.
The parent of your mother or father.
  - Each grandparent came to watch the school play.

**grandmother**: `/ˈɡrænmʌðə(r)/` - `n. [C]`  
  - His **paternal grandmother** lives in York.
  - She visits her grandmother **every weekend**.
The mother of your mother or father.
  - My grandmother taught me how to bake bread.

**grandfather**: `/ˈɡrænfɑːðə(r)/` - `n. [C]`  
  - Her **maternal grandfather** taught her chess.
  - My grandfather is **in his eighties**.
The father of your mother or father.
  - Her grandfather served in the navy when he was young.

**grandma**: `/ˈɡrænmɑː/` - `n. [C]`  
  - We are going to **Grandma's house** today.
  - Grandma baked a **birthday cake**.
An informal word for grandmother.
  - We visit Grandma every Sunday afternoon.

**granny**: `/ˈɡræni/` - `n. [C]`  
  - The children stayed with **Granny and Grandad**.
  - Granny tells us **bedtime stories**.
An informal word for grandmother.
  - The children spent the weekend at Granny's house.

**grandad**: `/ˈɡrændæd/` - `n. [C]`  
  - Grandad took us for a **walk in the park**.
  - We visited **Grandad's house** on Sunday.
An informal word for grandfather.
  - Grandad takes us fishing whenever we visit him.

**grandpa**: `/ˈɡrænpɑː/` - `n. [C]`  
  - Grandpa taught me to **ride a bike**.
  - We called Grandpa on **Father's Day**.
An informal word for grandfather.
  - His grandpa tells wonderful stories about the past.

**great-grandparent**: `/ˌɡreɪt ˈɡrænpeərənt/` - `n. [C]`  
  - She never met her **great-grandparents**.
  - The family tree includes four **generations of great-grandparents and descendants**.
The parent of one of your grandparents.
  - She found a photograph of her great-grandparent in an old album.

**great-grandmother**: `/ˌɡreɪt ˈɡrænmʌðə(r)/` - `n. [C]`  
  - Her great-grandmother kept a **family diary**.
  - They found a photograph of their **great-grandmother**.
The mother of one of your grandparents.
  - My great-grandmother lived in the village all her life.

**great-grandfather**: `/ˌɡreɪt ˈɡrænfɑːðə(r)/` - `n. [C]`  
  - His great-grandfather served in the **First World War**.
  - They named the baby after his **great-grandfather**.
The father of one of your grandparents.
  - Their great-grandfather emigrated to Britain in 1910.

## Grandchildren

**grandchild**: `/ˈɡræntʃaɪld/` - `n. [C]`  
  - They welcomed their **first grandchild**.
  - She looks after her grandchild **once a week**.
The child of your son or daughter.
  - Their first grandchild was born in May.

**grandson**: `/ˈɡrænsʌn/` - `n. [C]`  
  - Her grandson came to **visit for the weekend**.
  - He is their **eldest grandson**.
The son of your son or daughter.
  - Her grandson calls her every evening.

**granddaughter**: `/ˈɡrændɔːtə(r)/` - `n. [C]`  
  - Their granddaughter won a **school prize**.
  - My granddaughter is **learning to read**.
The daughter of your son or daughter.
  - My granddaughter is learning to play the piano.

**great-grandchild**: `/ˌɡreɪt ˈɡræntʃaɪld/` - `n. [C]`  
  - He was delighted to meet his **first great-grandchild**.
  - The photograph shows three **generations of the family**.
The child of one of your grandchildren.
  - He lived long enough to meet his first great-grandchild.

**great-grandson**: `/ˌɡreɪt ˈɡrænsʌn/` - `n. [C]`  
  - Her great-grandson visited at **Christmas time**.
  - He is the family's **youngest great-grandson**.
The son of one of your grandchildren.
  - Her great-grandson inherited the family watch.

**great-granddaughter**: `/ˌɡreɪt ˈɡrændɔːtə(r)/` - `n. [C]`  
  - Their great-granddaughter sent a **birthday card**.
  - She met her great-granddaughter **for the first time**.
The daughter of one of your grandchildren.
  - Their great-granddaughter visited them during the holidays.

## Parent's Siblings

**aunt**: `/ɑːnt/` - `n. [C]`  
  - My aunt is **on my mother's side**.
  - We went to visit **Aunt Jane**.
The sister of your mother or father, or the wife of your uncle.
  - My aunt lives near the coast.

**auntie**: `/ˈɑːnti/` - `n. [C]`  
  - Auntie is coming **round for tea**.
  - I spent the holidays with **Auntie Sue**.
An informal word for aunt.
  - Auntie Sue is coming to dinner tonight.

**uncle**: `/ˈʌŋkl/` - `n. [C]`  
  - My uncle lives **on the other side of town**.
  - We visited **Uncle Peter** at the weekend.
The brother of your mother or father, or the husband of your aunt.
  - His uncle taught him how to ride a bicycle.

## Sibling's Children

**niece**: `/niːs/` - `n. [C]`  
  - She is my **youngest niece**.
  - My niece is **starting school** in September.
The daughter of your brother or sister; it can also refer to the daughter of your spouse's sibling.
  - My niece starts secondary school this autumn.

**nephew**: `/ˈnefjuː/` - `n. [C]`  
  - He bought a present for his **young nephew**.
  - My nephew loves **playing football**.
The son of your brother or sister; it can also refer to the son of your spouse's sibling.
  - She bought a football for her nephew.

## Cousins

**cousin**: `/ˈkʌzn/` - `n. [C]`  
  - I went on holiday with my **cousins**.
  - She's my **first cousin** on my father's side.
A child of your aunt or uncle.
  - I spent the summer with my cousin.

**first cousin**: `/ˌfɜːst ˈkʌzn/` - `n. [C]`  
  - My first cousin is **the same age as me**.
  - They are **first cousins once removed**.
A child of your aunt or uncle; a cousin who shares a grandparent with you.
  - My first cousin and I have the same grandparents.

**second cousin**: `/ˌsekənd ˈkʌzn/` - `n. [C]`  
  - We met our second cousins at a **family reunion**.
  - They are **second cousins on their mother's side**.
A person who shares a great-grandparent with you, typically the child of your parent's first cousin.
  - At the reunion, I met a second cousin whose great-grandparents were also mine.
