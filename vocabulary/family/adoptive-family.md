# Adoptive Family

**adoptive parent**: `/əˈdɒptɪv ˈpeərənt/` - `n. [C]`  
A person who legally becomes the parent of a child through adoption.
  - Her adoptive parents supported her throughout her childhood.
  - The child was placed with **prospective adoptive parents**.

**biological parent**: `/ˌbaɪəˈlɒdʒɪkl ˈpeərənt/` - `n. [C]`  
A parent who is biologically related to a child.
  - The child was able to meet her biological parents when she turned eighteen.
  - She wanted to learn about her **biological parents**.

**birth parent**: `/ˈbɜːθ peərənt/` - `n. [C]`  
A biological parent of an adopted child, especially when distinguishing them from the adoptive parent.
  - The adoption records helped him learn more about his birth parents.
  - The agency helped him contact his **birth parents**.

**birth mother**: `/ˈbɜːθ mʌðə(r)/` - `n. [C]`  
A woman who gives birth to a child, especially when the child is later adopted by someone else.
  - She wrote a letter to the child she had placed for adoption as a birth mother.
  - She decided to meet her **birth mother**.
  - The letter was addressed to the child's **birth mother**.

**birth father**: `/ˈbɜːθ fɑːðə(r)/` - `n. [C]`  
A child's biological father, especially when the child is adopted by someone else.
  - The adopted man eventually found his birth father.
  - He eventually found his **birth father**.
  - The records contained information about her **birth father**.

**adopt**: `/əˈdɒpt/` - `v. [T]`  
To become the legal parent of another person's child.
  - They decided to adopt a child who needed a permanent home.
  - They hope to **adopt a child** next year.
  - The couple decided to **adopt a baby**.
  - They were approved to **adopt internationally**.

**adopted**: `/əˈdɒptɪd/` - `adj.`  
Having become part of a family through adoption.
  - He grew up with two adopted brothers.
  - She is their **adopted daughter**.
  - He grew up in an **adopted family**.

**adoptee**: `/ˌædɒpˈtiː/` - `n. [C]`  
A person who has been adopted.
  - The adoptee requested access to her original birth records.

**adoption**: `/əˈdɒpʃn/` - `n. [U]`  
The legal process through which someone becomes the parent of a child who is not their biological child.
  - The adoption was finalised after several months of legal proceedings.
  - The **adoption process** took several months.
  - They completed the **legal adoption** last spring.
  - The agency provides **adoption services**.

**adoptive**: `/əˈdɒptɪv/` - `adj.`  
Related to adoption or describing a person or family that adopts a child.
  - She has a close relationship with her adoptive family.
  - She has a close **adoptive family**.
  - The child visited his **adoptive parents**.

## Practice: Dialogue, Monologue and Reading

### 1. Dialogue: Talking About Adoption

**Maya:** Is Daniel your biological son?

**Ruth:** No, he's our adopted son. We became his **adoptive parents** when he was three.

**Maya:** Do you know anything about his **birth parents**?

**Ruth:** We know a little about his **birth mother** and **birth father**. We want him to understand his background, but we'll let him decide when he's ready to talk about it.

**Maya:** That sounds thoughtful. How does Daniel feel about being an **adoptee**?

**Ruth:** He's comfortable with the word **adopted**, and we talk openly about **adoption**. We explain that to **adopt** a child is to become their legal parent, but it doesn't erase their earlier story.

**Maya:** And “**adoptive**” describes the family relationship, right?

**Ruth:** Exactly. He's part of our family in every way that matters.

### 2. Monologue: Our Family Story

When my partner and I decided to **adopt**, we spent a long time learning what adoption involves. Becoming **adoptive parents** was not a quick decision; we wanted to be ready to support a child emotionally as well as practically. Our son is **adopted**, and we make sure he knows that being an **adoptee** is one part of his story, not his whole identity. We respect what we know about his **biological parents**, including his **birth mother** and **birth father**, and we answer his questions honestly. For us, **adoption** created a family while still leaving room for him to be curious about his origins.

### 3. Reading: Understanding Adoption

**Adoption** is the legal process through which a person becomes the parent of a child who is not their biological child. An **adoptive parent** assumes parental rights and responsibilities, while a **biological parent** is related to the child by birth. The terms **birth mother** and **birth father** specifically refer to the child's parents by birth and are often used when discussing a child's background. A child who has been adopted may be called an **adoptee**. The adjective **adoptive** describes a relationship created through adoption, as in “adoptive family.” Families use different approaches when talking about adoption, but open, age-appropriate conversations can help children understand their personal histories without feeling that they must choose between their birth connections and their adoptive family.
