# Guardianship

**guardian**: `/ˈɡɑːdiən/` - `n. [C]`  
A person who is legally responsible for caring for a child or another person who cannot fully manage their own affairs.
  - The court appointed her aunt as her legal guardian.
  - The court appointed her uncle as her **legal guardian**.
  - She became the child's **appointed guardian**.

---

## Practice: Dialogue, Monologue and Reading

### 1. Dialogue: Choosing a Guardian

**Helen:** Have you decided who would care for the children if you couldn't?

**Mark:** We're discussing who could become their **guardian**. We want someone who knows them well and can provide a stable home.

**Helen:** Would that person automatically get legal authority?

**Mark:** Not necessarily. **Guardianship** depends on the law and the arrangements in place, so we're getting proper legal advice before making a decision.

### 2. Monologue: Planning for the Children

Choosing a **guardian** is a serious decision because it concerns a child's care and welfare if their parents are unable to look after them. We are thinking about the person's relationship with our children, their ability to provide a stable environment, and whether they understand our values. We also know that naming someone informally may not be enough to establish legal **guardianship**, so we intend to follow the proper process in our jurisdiction.

### 3. Reading: Guardianship and Responsibility

A **guardian** is a person legally responsible for caring for a child or another person who cannot fully manage their own affairs. **Guardianship** refers to the legal relationship or arrangement that gives the guardian particular duties and, where applicable, decision-making authority. The scope of those responsibilities differs between jurisdictions and may be determined by a court or another legal process. A guardian's role should be understood in the context of the person's welfare and the applicable law; informal promises and legal appointment are not necessarily the same thing.
