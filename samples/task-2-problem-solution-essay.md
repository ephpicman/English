# IELTS General Training Writing Task 2 — Problem-and-Solution Essay

## Practice prompt

In many towns and cities, people rely heavily on private cars for everyday journeys. What problems does this cause, and what measures could governments and individuals take to address them?

Write at least 250 words. You should spend about 40 minutes on this task.

## Model answer

In many urban areas, private cars remain the preferred way to travel, even for relatively short journeys. This dependence contributes to environmental damage and makes cities less convenient to live in. Although the problem cannot be solved overnight, governments and individuals can reduce it by making alternatives more practical and by reconsidering when a car is genuinely necessary.

One major consequence is congestion. When large numbers of people drive to work at similar times, roads become crowded, journeys take longer and buses are delayed as well. This wastes time and fuel, while exhaust emissions worsen local air quality. Heavy traffic can also make streets less attractive for walking or cycling, creating a cycle in which people feel they need a car even for short trips. In addition, households that depend on driving face ongoing costs for fuel, parking, insurance and maintenance, which can place pressure on lower-income budgets.

Governments should therefore provide reliable alternatives before expecting people to abandon their cars. Frequent buses and trains, integrated ticketing and safe cycle routes can make public transport and cycling realistic options. Employers could support this shift by offering flexible start times or allowing staff to work remotely when their duties permit it, thereby reducing peak-hour demand. Urban planners can also place housing, shops and essential services closer together so that everyday errands do not require a long journey.

Individuals have a role too. Where safe and practical, they can walk, cycle, share journeys or combine several errands into one trip. However, these choices are not equally available to everyone: people with disabilities, shift workers and residents of poorly connected areas may have few alternatives. Policies should recognise these constraints rather than treating car use simply as a matter of personal responsibility.

In conclusion, excessive reliance on private cars increases congestion, pollution and household costs. A combination of dependable public transport, better urban design and realistic individual choices offers the most effective way to reduce these problems fairly.

## Why this is a strong response

- **Task Response:** It explains the problems and offers several feasible measures, linking them directly to the causes described.
- **Coherence and Cohesion:** The response follows a clear problem-to-solution structure, with a separate paragraph acknowledging practical limitations.
- **Lexical Resource:** Vocabulary such as “integrated ticketing,” “peak-hour demand,” and “poorly connected areas” is specific to the topic and used naturally.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy:** The essay uses complex clauses, conditionals, participle phrases and contrast structures while keeping the meaning clear.
